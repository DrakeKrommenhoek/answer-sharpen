# Should a member ever tap the word "shame"?

**The one question Drake left open on 2026-09-11: "need to explore this deeper
and figure it out."** This is that exploration. It ends with a recommendation
and the argument against it.

---

## The question, precisely

Joe's six transformations are the engine of the Emotional Net Worth Plan:

> shame → self-acceptance · blame → personal responsibility · guilt →
> forgiveness · fear → faith · anger → purposeful power · resentment → freedom

The plan's first step, Assess, asks *"which feelings desire compassionate
attention?"* Answering that inside the app means a member selecting one of those
six words about themselves.

So: **are the six an input a member gives, or a teaching a member receives?**

---

## What makes this hard

**The naming is the mechanism.** Joe's whole claim is that an emotion carries
information and that meeting it by name is what turns it into an asset. A
practice that refuses to say the words has removed the thing that works.

**And the naming is the risk.** A member alone at five in the morning, tapping a
chip that says *shame* about themselves, with nobody present and nothing that
follows, has been handed a label rather than a transformation. The app's own
posture is the opposite of that: Return Practice, the grace days, the three rest
doorways, and a partner card that has no way to show absence all exist so a
member is met rather than measured.

**Three of the six are Tier 2 words**, which is the language rule noticing the
same tension from the other side.

---

## Four ways through

### A. Member-facing, daily

A second half of the daily assess: *"Which feeling is ready for loving
attention?"*, six chips.

**For:** the most complete version of Joe's step one. Daily contact with the
framework.

**Against:** it asks a member to self-label a hard emotion every morning, alone,
at the exact moment the practice is meant to be fifteen low-friction minutes.
Daily self-labelling is also the shape most likely to become identity rather
than weather: *I am someone who picks shame.*

### B. Member-facing, weekly, with the anchor present

The six appear in the Circle review, where Joe's five questions already live and
where a human being is in the room.

**For:** matches step six exactly. Matches the research on disclosure, which is
consistent that naming something heavy wants a named person nearby. Weekly is
also the cadence a hard feeling can actually be worked, rather than merely
logged.

**Against:** weekly contact only, and it depends on circles existing, which
depends on the backend and the anchors. It puts the most valuable half of Joe's
framework behind the part that is furthest from shipping.

### C. Teaching frame only

The six live in Joe's content, in the library, in the intro. The app teaches the
pairs and never asks a member to select one.

**For:** zero risk, and it is honest about what an app can hold. The
transformations do their work as understanding rather than as data.

**Against:** it gives up the assess entirely. A framework nobody applies to
today is a poster.

### D. Enter from the right-hand side

The member never taps the feeling. They tap **what they want to grow**:

> self-acceptance · personal responsibility · forgiveness · faith · purposeful
> power · freedom

Same six pairs, entered from the destination. Joe's teaching supplies the left
side: *this is what self-acceptance grows out of.*

**For:**
- The member names a direction rather than a diagnosis. *I could use forgiveness
  today* and *I am carrying guilt* point at the same place, and only one of them
  is a label.
- It is affirmative by construction, so the language rule stops being a tension
  and becomes a description. The member-facing surface never shows the three
  Tier 2 words at all, and the carve-out shrinks to the teaching layer.
- **It is the same shape as the assess already designed.** "What grew today"
  taps an asset. "What is ready to grow" taps an asset. One interaction, two
  directions, and the balance sheet has both columns.
- It survives a member who is unwilling to say the hard word yet, which is most
  people most of the time, and which is usually the same person who most needs
  the transformation.

**Against, and this is real:** it may be a softening of exactly the thing Joe
meant. His framework says meeting the feeling *by name* is the work. Entering
from the destination could let a member skate over the meeting. Joe is the one
who can say whether naming the wanting is enough, or whether the practice has to
say the harder word out loud to do anything.

---

## Recommendation

**D for the daily surface, B for the weekly one, and C underneath both.**

A member taps what is ready to grow, every day, one tap, beside the tap for what
grew. In the weekly Circle review, with an anchor present, the fuller question
can be asked in Joe's own words, because that is where a person is there to
receive the answer. And the six pairs are taught in full, in Joe's voice, so a
member always knows what their chosen destination grows out of.

That gives the balance sheet two columns of daily data, keeps the transformations
whole as a teaching, and puts the harder naming in the one place where someone is
listening.

**The strongest argument against it** is the one above, and it is Joe's to
settle: if naming the feeling by name is the mechanism, then a practice that
only ever names the destination has kept the vocabulary and quietly dropped the
work. I think entering from the right is a doorway rather than an exit, because
a member who taps *forgiveness* six days running has been told plainly, in the
teaching, what forgiveness grows from. But I hold that loosely, and Joe has been
doing this with people for far longer than this app has existed.

---

## What this does NOT decide

Whether the daily assess should ask both halves at once, or alternate. Two taps
on a closing card is already twice the friction of one, and the constraint the
whole practice lives under is that starting stays easy. Worth watching once the
first column has real data behind it.
